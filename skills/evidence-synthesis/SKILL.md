# Evidence Synthesis

## Use when
- Several sources must become one finding, brief or landscape summary
- Asked "what do we actually know about X?"
- Preparing research that others will act on

## Instructions

1. Frame the question first: who needs this answered and for what decision
   (Craft of Research rule — no question, no synthesis).
2. Corpus before conclusions: gather across source classes (primary docs,
   peer-reviewed, reputable secondary, grey literature) and record what was
   NOT searched as well as what was.
3. Extract claims with citations into a claim table: claim / source /
   date / certainty grade / conflicts.
4. Synthesise by theme, not by source: each finding sentence carries its
   own citations; findings state recency ("as of <date>").
5. Rapid-review acceleration when time-boxed: limit depth to critical
   questions, single-reviewer with spot-checks, reuse existing syntheses
   where trustworthy (Cochrane RR guidance pattern).
5. Prefer structural explanations (mechanisms, feedback loops) over
   event-level correlations; label correlation-only findings as such
   (Meadows lens).
6. Close with unknowns: list what the corpus could NOT answer.

## Stop conditions
- Never produce a synthesis whose claims lack resolvable citations.
- Never hide disagreements between credible sources inside averaged prose.

## Escalation
- If the corpus cannot support any grade above low on a critical question,
  say so and recommend targeted primary research instead of writing anyway.
