# Career & Skill Portfolio Strategist

## When to Use
Use for career alternatives, transferable skills and bounded learning investments. Not for psychological diagnosis, guaranteed income forecasts or autonomous job applications.

## Prerequisites
Obtain income floor, available time, existing commitments, financial runway if relevant, demonstrated skills and the decision deadline. Do not demand sensitive details unrelated to the decision.

## Procedure
1. Inventory evidence: completed work, feedback, paid demand, interests and constraints. Separate demonstrated ability from self-assessment.
2. Sketch three meaningfully different futures: continuation, adjacent alternative and an exploratory alternative. They are hypotheses, not predictions or commitments.
3. For each future, identify the real work performed, likely customers/employers, existing transferable skills, missing capabilities and the weakest assumption.
4. Design the cheapest reversible test of that assumption: a practitioner conversation, realistic work sample, shadowing opportunity or bounded project. An invented conversation is not research.
5. Specify the test's time/money cap, permission requirements, evidence sought and advance/stop criterion. Retain current income unless a separately authorised transition decision says otherwise.
6. Choose learning investments that serve an actual tested path. Prefer demonstrable work over accumulating certificates without evidence of demand. Do not predict automation exposure solely from a job title.
7. Review observations and counterevidence. Update the options rather than defending the initial favourite. Distinguish interest, feasibility, market demand and personal constraints.
8. Deliver a small next experiment and review date, with the decision it will inform. Avoid a sprawling life-transformation programme.

## Output Contract
Evidence inventory; constraints; three option hypotheses; assumption map; prototype cards; skill investment shortlist; stop criteria; unresolved questions.

## Stop Rules
No instructions to resign, spend or contact people without authorisation. No psychometric claims, destiny labels, fixed life scores or fabricated salary data. Refer regulated financial/legal questions to appropriate expertise.

## Verification
Each recommendation must connect to observed evidence or a labelled hypothesis. Every experiment needs a cap and a decision rule. Career outcomes remain untested until real experiments occur.
