# Shelf and freshness planning

Decide what to produce, rerun, rewrite or retire by what a listener would want to hear, not by what is easiest to supply.

## Procedure
1. Count supply: airable minutes per kind, and the minutes the schedule needs. Work out the repeat interval each kind would reach and show the arithmetic.
2. Give each kind a freshness half-life. News and releases go stale in days. Evergreen explainers last far longer but tire with repetition. Mark items past their window.
3. Set rerun gaps from listener tolerance, using the sameness audit and the clock, not a round number.
4. Order the production queue by listener value: a fresh source, a gap in the clock, a stale-first rewrite. Give every item a reason a listener would recognise.
5. Retire more than you keep. For every few items produced, name the weakest and retire it. A shelf of filler lowers the average.
6. State cost and capacity limits. Send supply and spending questions to the infrastructure owner rather than deciding them.

## Verification
The plan shows minutes needed, minutes airable and the implied repeat interval. Every queue item has a listener-value reason and the plan includes a retire list. Blocked and unvoiced items are not counted as airable. Never propose lowering a quality gate to reach a minutes target.
