# Positioning and search diagnosis

Sources: G05, G02. G03 identifies the book; it is not a substitute for reading it. This is original distillation of the author's public method and official documentation.

## Positioning dependency chain
Begin with the customer's actual alternative, not every theoretical competitor. A spreadsheet, manual process or no purchase can be the primary alternative. Compare differentiated capabilities only against those options. Explain the customer outcome each capability enables. Find the segment that especially values those outcomes. Choose the category that makes that value understandable.

Do not begin with a preferred tagline and work backwards to manufacture support. Separate real deal/interview evidence from untested positioning hypotheses. Creating a new category demands customer education; novelty is not automatically a competitive advantage.

## Search acquisition diagnostic
Inspect crawl/index access before rewriting copy. Inspect information organisation and meaningful links before producing more pages. Map a page to a real user need, then examine whether its title, headings, body and search presentation help satisfy that need. Useful content and clear organisation are the objective; keyword repetition is not.

Use search-console or equivalent actual observations where authorised. If unavailable, report what can be inspected from the public site and what cannot. Do not infer impressions, rank, query volume or revenue from page appearance.

## Boundary with existing roles
Product management retains product priorities and pricing decisions. Content production writes approved assets. This persona connects a diagnosed growth problem to a testable intervention and measurement design; it does not absorb those roles.

## Excluded claims
No guarantees of Google indexing, top rankings, AI-search citations, conversion uplift or one-person-company income. No simulated interviews passed off as customer research.
