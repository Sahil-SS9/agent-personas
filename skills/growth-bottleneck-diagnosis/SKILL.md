# Growth Bottleneck Diagnosis

## When to Use
Use for a growth decision with funnel or customer evidence. Do not absorb copy production or general product ownership.

## Inputs
Name the decision owner, objective, horizon, population, unit, event definitions, exclusions, cohort maturity and data sources. An event count is not necessarily a person count. Separate unique organisations, unique people, sessions and page views.

## Procedure
1. Inventory each source and its observation window. Mark supplied examples synthetic. Never manufacture customer interviews, traffic or competitor prices.
2. Calculate rates only when numerator and denominator refer to the same eligible cohort and outcome window. Use the supplied calculator or another verified tool. If a cohort has not matured, label the rate provisional; do not compare it with a matured cohort as churn or deterioration.
3. Inspect acquisition mix and the full path before choosing a conversion button. A large percentage drop is a candidate constraint, not automatic proof of root cause or highest economic leverage.
4. Triangulate observed losses with customer alternatives, support records and direct task observations. Volunteer interviews can suggest mechanisms but cannot estimate population prevalence. Explicitly record counterevidence and sample-selection limits.
5. Compare at least two plausible explanations. Choose one small, reversible intervention whose result could change the decision. Prefer correcting a demonstrated failure over buying more exposure to it. Do not diagnose pricing solely from aggregate conversion.
6. Pass a bounded hypothesis, target population and uncertainty to experiment-feasibility-review. Reuse market research only if market evidence is genuinely missing; do not import its historical example prices or task-filing defaults.

## Output Contract
Evidence IDs; cohort-safe rates; candidate bottleneck; alternative explanations; chosen intervention; expected mechanism; falsifier; owner; time/cash ceiling; next observation. Label proposed costs as budgets, not incurred expenditure.

## Stop Rules
Unknown denominators or conflicting event definitions block causal interpretation. Missing context triggers specific questions, not invented values. Spend, outreach and rollout require separate authority.

## Verification
Recompute rates from named inputs. Trace every observation to a source. The recommendation must explain what evidence would overturn it.
