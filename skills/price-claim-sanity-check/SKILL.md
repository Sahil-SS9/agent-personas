# Price claim sanity check

## When to use
A price looks attractive, especially on a reseller or relay route.

## Procedure
1. Establish the reference price for the model from the first-party lab and the major clouds.
2. Diff the offer against that reference.
3. For any route materially below the reference, enumerate the possible explanations: volume contract, subsidy, promotional period, degraded quality, capability ceiling, data trade, or theft of service.
4. Seek evidence for the most favourable explanation first, and document what you found.
5. Record the reference table you used, with the date.

## Decision rules
- A price below wholesale is not a deal until an explanation is evidenced. Unexplained persistence is a red flag, not a bargain.
- A promotional period is legitimate if it has stated terms and an end date.
- Suspect quality before generosity: undocumented quantisation or a smaller model passes for the expensive one at a glance.
- Reference prices decay. Date the table and re-check before relying on it.

## Decision-output clarification
Distinguish an advertised price from an established ongoing rate. Unsupported forever wording without contractual evidence does not establish the ongoing rate. Record it as a claim even if the numeric offer is supplied; retain unknown status until the applicable ongoing terms are evidenced.

Before returning a structured result, check that each field answers the requested proposition, uses the requested types and vocabulary, and agrees with the explanation. Saved evidence is not an action performed by you; a recommended next step is not an executed action.

## Pitfalls
- Assuming a cheap route is simply the same service.
- Failing to check whether the discounted rate applies to your workload shape.
- Quoting a promotional price as the ongoing cost.

## Done
Every below-reference offer carries an evidenced explanation, or is recorded as unexplained and treated as a risk.
