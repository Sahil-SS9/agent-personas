# Humour from specifics

Make a script lively without adding anything the evidence does not contain. A joke here is colour on top of a cited claim: it never replaces one and never adds one.

## Procedure
1. Before writing, pick the one or two most concrete details in the cited sections: an odd number, a limit, a name, an order of steps. Write each down with its citation. If the evidence has nothing concrete, say so and write it straight. A flat script is better than an invented joke.
2. Say the detail as a picture, not an abstraction ("memorize specific and colorful details. There is nothing funny about the abstract", Geller). The picture may restate or compare the cited fact. It may not add a behaviour, cause, number, name or mechanism the evidence does not state.
3. Test every joke by deleting it. The claim line must still carry the same fact, and the cited line must still say everything the joke seemed to say. A joke that is the only place a fact appears is a claim: cite it or cut it. Never dress a claim as colour.
4. Give the foil a real objection that a cited line can answer. The foil pushes on a point in the evidence ("so I cannot see what is running?") and the other host answers from the source. A reply that only repeats or paraphrases the last line is not a reaction; weak co-hosts "repeat or paraphrase points" (Radio Ink). Let the foil concede once, late, in its own words.
5. Aim every joke at the idea, the tool or the situation. Never at a person, and never at Hermes. A host has no past: no "last week", no "my coworkers", no "a panic I have had". A joke at the host's own expense may use only what the cast file already says about that host.
6. Spend jokes sparingly. As a starting heuristic, one or two per break, one at the turn and one after the listener has learned something. A callback may reuse an earlier image from the same script once. A joke wants a beat: mark the line for the on-air performance director instead of writing stage directions.
7. Run the station checks tool, then read every joke yourself against the delete-it test. The persona check can flag a harmless what-if and miss a joke about a host's past.
8. Hand the scripts over without grading them. Whether they are funny is for the Programme Director and a listener.

## Verification
Every joke is tied to a cited detail. Deleting any joke leaves every claim line unchanged. No joke adds a fact, a number, a name or a behaviour. The foil raises at least one objection that a cited line answers. No host claims a personal history and nothing roasts Hermes or a person. No verdict on how funny the script is is given.
