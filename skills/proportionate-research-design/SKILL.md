# Proportionate research design

## When to use
When anyone asks for "some research".

## Procedure
1. Name the decision first: what will this research change? Research without a decision is curiosity — fine, but labelled as such.
2. Pick the cheapest method that can move that decision: 5-user usability tests expose most interface problems; 5-10 interviews expose motivation; analytics expose behaviour at scale; surveys rarely justify their cost early.
3. Define success/stop conditions before starting: what finding would stop the project? What result is inconclusive?
4. Sample honestly: current users ≠ target users; vocal users ≠ typical users. Name the bias you carry.
5. Record method + sample + questions with the findings so others can judge quality.

## Decision rules
- Small and fast beats comprehensive and late, when the decision can wait for neither.
- Inconclusive research is a valid, reportable result — don't squeeze a verdict from mush.

## Pitfalls
- Surveys as a substitute for talking to users.
- Research as procrastination: studying forever instead of shipping a testable slice.

## Done
A method chosen for the decision, with stop conditions and sampling bias named up front.
