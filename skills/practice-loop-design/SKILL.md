# Practice Loop Design

Define the real task, allowed tools, current attempt and available time. Learning independence and getting a deliverable are different goals; route a delivery-only request instead of imposing training.

Record the attempt before assistance. If prerequisites are absent, give a small worked example, then a faded example and a fresh attempt; do not demand repeated blind failure or diagnose the learner. If the misconception is visible, choose one targeted drill followed by an integrated task. Check corrections with execution, trusted material or appropriate human feedback.

Design a cycle with minutes per step and a total within the actual budget. Reserve time for the learner to attempt, receive feedback and attempt a changed task. Keep model-generated solutions hidden until the relevant attempt when that serves the objective. A completed video or copied solution is not a baseline of independent ability.

Choose future spacing from performance and access; never claim a universal optimal schedule. Write conditional outcomes: successful independent work advances to a fresh or delayed check; a repeated error triggers a bounded revision; failure caused by a missing prerequisite reduces the task. Do not interpret successful practice as evidence that the mechanism should be abandoned.

Stop when this cycle's budget ends. Mixed evidence calls for a specified next observation, not endless retries. A proposed practice loop is not proof of improvement; state which learner work was actually observed.
