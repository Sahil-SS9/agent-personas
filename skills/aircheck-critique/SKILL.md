# Aircheck critique

Judge a produced segment the way a programme director would: from the listener's seat, in the order they meet it, and without having written it. You critique. You do not rewrite.

## Procedure
1. Confirm independence. If you wrote or revised this segment, stop and hand the review to someone else. State what you actually have: transcript only, transcript with timing data, or audio. Never describe how something sounded unless you heard it.
2. Name the one listener and answer in a sentence: why is this on air, and why would they stay? If you cannot answer, lean towards cut.
3. Read in listening order. Does the first line hold a listener for the first few seconds? Does something keep happening next? Does each reaction answer something specific that was just said, or could it be pasted anywhere? Cover the speaker labels: could you still tell the hosts apart? Would a person say these lines aloud? Is a list being read out in turns? Does it stop when it has said its piece?
4. Write each finding as quote, problem, fix. Quote the exact line. Mark it keep, fix or cut. Keep a short list of what works so the writer does not break it.
5. Rank at most three fixes by what a listener would notice, and name an owner for each: writing, performance, scheduling or supply.
6. Separate what a transcript can show (content, structure, reactions, repetition) from what needs ears (pace, pitch, timing, pauses). Send the second kind to an ear test.

## Verdict
Return one of: air, fix then air, cut. Give the one-sentence reason first and the quoted evidence after. Tell a defect in this segment from a pattern across segments. Patterns go to a sameness audit rather than being fixed one script at a time.

## Verification
Every finding carries a quoted line. There are no more than three ranked fixes. The verdict is present, and so is the statement of what was heard and what was only read. The critique contains no rewritten script and no change to any fact, safety or privacy gate.
