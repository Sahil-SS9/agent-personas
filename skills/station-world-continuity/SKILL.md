# Station world continuity

Make the station feel lived-in from a ledger it actually holds, never from a made-up past. Hosts have no history, so what regular listeners recognise must come from the station: the cast file's running bits, rivalries and catchphrase limits, and any callback or correction the brief hands over. Nothing here may add a fact, a number, a date or an earlier show.

## Procedure
1. Find the ledger. It is only what the brief supplies for this pair: each host's running bits and catchphrase limits, any list of recent callbacks, any storyline or recorded correction. If it supplies none, write no cross-show continuity and go to step 7. Do not rebuild a ledger from memory of other scripts.
2. Use at most one running bit, and only when it fits: it must meet something the cited evidence says in this script (a local-versus-cloud rivalry where the evidence is about running models locally). If it needs a stretch, a new fact or a forced change of topic, leave it out.
3. Write a bit as a standing habit, not a dated event. The hosts can bicker in their usual way; they cannot count it. No tally figure, ordinal ("the ninth week"), date or "last time" unless the ledger states it. A tally may be named as the bit the cast file names, with no number: a figure turns colour into a claim, and a claim needs a source.
4. Keep the station's limits. A line with no source is banter, and banter inside an explainer fails the segment, so in an explainer the bit rides on a cited claim line (a "Well, actually" that carries the cited fact) or waits for a segment that allows banter. Use a catchphrase once at most, inside its hourly limit, and never a banned phrase.
5. Call back only to what the ledger holds, in the ledger's words, adding nothing about what was said or who was right. A recorded retcon (old, new, reason) may be told as "that changed". With no list, call back inside the script: a word, an image or an open question from earlier in it, once. Never invent an earlier mistake ("I got that wrong last time") or an earlier show.
6. Aim every bit at ideas. A rivalry or an award ("worst take of the week") goes to an idea in this script, never to a named contributor, a real company or a person, and never to Hermes. A theory is a host's opinion in so many words, never a fact, and accuses no one.
7. Write for a listener who has just tuned in. Give the bit a clause the first time it appears, naming the host by what they do, so the regular gets the nod and the newcomer is not lost: "Since new people keep tuning in" the host has to say who is who (Geller).
8. Run the station checks tool and fix what it flags. Hand the scripts over with a list of which ledger items were used and which were left out, and why. Do not grade the result.

## Verification
Every bit and callback is in the ledger, or is a callback to an earlier line of the same script. At most one running bit. No tally figure, ordinal, date or "last time" the ledger does not state. In an explainer the bit sits on a cited line. No catchphrase over its limit, no banned phrase. Every rivalry or award aims at an idea. No invented correction or earlier show. The hand-over lists which ledger items were used and which were left out.
