# Design discovery and comparison

Authority: user-approved candidate revision. This is an operating method, not empirical proof of improved taste.

## Adaptive interview
Inspect the existing brief first. Ask one unresolved question at a time, using plain contrasts: calm versus expressive, information-dense versus spacious, photographic versus typographic. Ask for liked/disliked references and which elements matter, not just a brand name. Establish audience, task, brand preservation and practical constraints. Do not ask the same question in different words. Users can say “choose for me”; record that authority and state assumptions.

## Direction contract
Default three options; five on request for broader exploration. Each has an identifier, design thesis, audience fit, information/composition structure, typography, palette, imagery, density, motion/interaction, source influences, implementation feasibility, main trade-off and desktop/mobile preview handles. Keep the same representative content and task so a prettier image or shorter copy does not rig the comparison. Do not enforce five predetermined aesthetic families on every project.

Combining influences means coherent synthesis: for example, editorial information hierarchy plus product-specific imagery plus restrained interactive feedback. It does not require combining three libraries or copying three sites. Design references are not licences to reproduce assets.

## Pairwise difference review
For each pair, state observable differences in composition, typographic hierarchy, imagery/art direction, density and interaction. A palette/font swap on the same skeleton fails. Require at least one meaningful composition/information-layout difference plus a second substantive difference; this is a review heuristic, not a scientific originality score. Check screenshots in context, not only metadata assertions. Mobile options must preserve their rationale, not collapse into identical generic stacks.

## Creative range without an effect recipe

Specify a coherent experience, not a quota of effects. A direction needs its own typographic voice (family, width, weight, scale and rhythm), spatial reading path, background/material treatment and task-specific interaction model. Texture, imagery, gradients, colour bleed, blend modes, SVG, canvas or shaders can shape the whole composition when the concept calls for them; none is mandatory on every job. Avoid a memorable hero attached to interchangeable bands. Do not replace one template with a fixed safe/bold/experimental trio for every brief.

Keep equivalent outcomes and representative content across directions, but allow different ways to reach them: direct service choice, an explainable guided finder, a comparison tool, spatial navigation or another appropriate mechanism. Prototype the live interaction that makes a concept distinctive. Custom does not mean unprecedented, and more steps are not automatically better UX. Keep a direct route available when exploration is optional.

Use a lawful, genuinely varied font/art pool when evaluating creative range. Give motion a purpose and rhythm beyond an entrance fade; show normal-motion behaviour and verify reduced motion separately. Review backgrounds, section relationships and mobile composition throughout the journey. A functioning interface can still fail human creative acceptance.

## Coherence and fidelity
Check the whole page or representative workflow. Components must share a deliberate token and interaction model. A striking hero followed by unrelated default sections fails coherence. Distinction that makes the primary task harder fails brief fidelity. Familiar patterns are often right for dense tools, public services and transaction flows.

## Presentation and selection
Show previews before long explanations. State the main trade-off and recommended fit. Ask for a chosen direction and targeted likes/dislikes. If users combine elements, describe exactly what transfers and render the recombination before calling it approved. Moodboards may support exploration but cannot silently replace rendered previews. Full functionality is not required in lightweight previews; label nonfunctional controls and sample data.

## Promotion evaluation plan
Use diverse held-out briefs: editorial, commerce, creative marketing, dense product UI, public service, non-React project and existing-brand preservation. Render candidate outputs and assess task completion, pairwise distinction, coherent desktop/mobile composition and user preference against a baseline. Include a conventional brief where forced novelty should lose. Human visual judgements are separate from automated a11y/build checks; this plan has not been run.
