# Claim Verification

## Use when
- A claim will enter a report, decision memo or published content
- Sources disagree and the disagreement matters
- A surprising statistic "everyone repeats" needs a primary origin

## Instructions

1. SIFT every load-bearing claim:
   - Stop: state what the claim supports; if it supports nothing, skip.
   - Investigate: leave the source; search what independent parties say
     about it (About pages are self-advertising).
   - Find better coverage: prefer the most authoritative coverage of the
     CLAIM over the first mention.
   - Trace: follow to the primary source; confirm quote/number/context
     survived re-publication.
2. Grade residual certainty per claim: high (primary + independent
   corroboration), medium (credible secondary only), low (single unverified
   source). State the grade inline in output.
3. Conflicting sources: report the conflict with both positions cited;
   never average them into false consensus.
4. Numeric claims get the statistics lens: sample size and selection,
   denominator, baseline, absolute-vs-relative framing, and who funded the
   underlying measurement (Huff/Spiegelhalter checklist).
5. Mark unresolved claims explicitly rather than smoothing prose.

## Stop conditions
- Never cite a source you have not opened beyond its headline/snippet.
- Never present an inference as a quoted fact.

## Escalation
- Claims that would drive costly decisions but stay low-certainty get
  flagged for human verification with what to check listed.
