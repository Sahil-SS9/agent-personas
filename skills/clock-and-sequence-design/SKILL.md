# Clock and sequence design

Treat a programme hour as a template of positions that software will fill again and again. A weak clock repeats its mistake perfectly every hour.

## Procedure
1. Place the walls first: the station or legal ID, fixed features and any hard commitments. Everything flexible fits around them.
2. Give each break one job and one thought. A break that needs two ideas is two breaks.
3. Plan the seams. Each item opens with something worth hearing or a payoff that was teased before the break, with a short signpost when the topic changes. Never tease what the next item cannot deliver. Give the writer the next item's title so a tease is possible.
4. Shape energy across the hour like a shift: strongest early, protect the middle, close with a ritual the listener recognises. Match the daypart.
5. Decide talk against music as a programming choice per daypart, not a flat quota.
6. Test the clock. Fill three consecutive hours from the real shelf and list adjacent items that share a shape, a host pair, a topic or an opening. Re-run after every change.

Ratings-driven stopset placement does not carry over where there are no ads or ratings. The listener-retention reasoning behind it does.

## Verification
The clock lists its walls, break jobs, teases with their payoff positions and an energy shape. The simulated hours name every adjacent repeat and how each is resolved. No rule is relaxed to make a clock fit.
