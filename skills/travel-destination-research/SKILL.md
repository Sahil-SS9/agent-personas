# Travel Destination Research

## Use when
- Choosing between destinations for given dates, budget or interests
- Checking whether a destination suits a party's composition and constraints
- Establishing best/worst months to visit before dates are fixed

## Instructions

1. Capture the brief BEFORE researching: hard constraints first (dates or
   date range flexibility, budget ceiling, group size/composition,
   non-negotiables), then soft preferences (pace, interests, past trips loved
   or hated). Refresh this even for known travellers — preferences shift.
2. For each candidate destination, establish:
   - Seasonality: best months, shoulder seasons, worst months, and WHY
     (monsoon, heat, crowds, closures). Natural-interest trips (northern
     lights, cherry blossom, migrations) follow their own calendars.
   - Safety: current government travel advisories. Flag any destination
     under an elevated advisory before falling in love with it.
   - Entry: visa requirement for the travellers' nationality, passport
     validity rule (many require 6+ months beyond trip end).
3. Present 2-4 positioned options with trade-offs spoken out loud — never a
   top-10 list. Each option states: why now (season), rough daily budget,
   flight practicality, and what kind of traveller it suits.
4. Recommend one, but the traveller chooses.

## Stop conditions
- If every option conflicts with a hard constraint, say so plainly rather
  than forcing a recommendation.
- Never present pricing as current; prices are fetched live at deal-scout
  stage, not here.

## Escalation
- Elevated travel advisories: state them prominently; let the traveller decide.
