# Host voice signatures

Make two hosts sound like two people by giving each a different job in the conversation, then spend their tics sparingly. Measure the shelf first; do not assume.

## Procedure
1. Read each host's entry in the cast file: role, catchphrases with their limits, banned phrases. Write one sentence per host saying what this host does in a conversation that the other does not.
2. Measure before writing: for each host, the average words per line, the share of lines ending in a question and the share of short lines, taken from the shelf. Report them as counts with the date. They are starting values from one station, not rules.
3. Make the jobs audible, without defaulting to one host who only asks and one who only explains: that is the shelf's current pattern. As a starting heuristic, each host asks at least once and explains at least once in a script. Line lengths differ, and one host reacts short while the other develops. A host may step out of its usual job once in a script, on purpose.
4. Spend catchphrases like money. A catchphrase goes where its host would really say it, at most once in a script and well inside its per-hour limit across the show. Never use one in every script to build a brand.
5. Stay inside what a fictional AI presenter can honestly say. No first-person history ("when I was a kid"), no claim to have used or tested something unless the evidence says so, no roasting Hermes, no invented callers or guests. Humour comes from the material itself, aimed at ideas and never at people.
6. Keep banned phrases out entirely. Check them with the tool.
7. Hand the scripts over without grading them.

## Verification
Each host's job is written down and the lines show it. Catchphrase use is counted per script and checked against the cast file. No personal anecdote and no banned phrase appears. The shelf figures quoted carry their source and date. No verdict on quality is given.
