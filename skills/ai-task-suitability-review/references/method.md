# Delegation evaluation

Sources: A01/A04 (NIST framework and playbook), A02 (research abstract), S01/S04 (discovery hypotheses). A03 is a restricted reference-only comparator; no Google PAIR text or adapted checklist is incorporated.

NIST frames AI risk work through Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. The playbook is voluntary and contextual, not a mandatory exhaustive checklist or certification. Use proportionate documentation that changes a real decision.

Microsoft's human-AI interaction research abstract establishes the design problem and evaluation scope; it does not establish performance of this candidate or any particular deployment.

## Authority matrix
For each task specify allowed inputs, tools, output destination, approval requirement, prohibited actions, evidence source, owner and fallback. Reversible drafting and irreversible transactions should not share an undifferentiated permission level.

## Cost accounting
Record setup separately from recurring costs. Compare at matched quality: baseline human time versus execution/supervision plus review and rework. Count machine latency separately when it does not consume human attention. Include cash cost, errors and missed deadlines where relevant. Do not double count parallel time or amortise setup across imaginary future volume.

Synthetic illustration: 60 minutes baseline, 10 minutes active AI supervision, 20 minutes review and 40 minutes rework means the AI path consumes more human effort. This is a hypothetical arithmetic example, not observed benchmark evidence.

## Appropriate evidence
Code: executed tests plus reviewed changes. Research: checked sources and claim alignment. External updates: read back the exact target. Creative drafts: actual acceptance criteria and human review where needed. A fluent summary cannot replace these checks.

## Risk response
When output verification is weak and consequences are high, retain human control. When a task is reversible, tightly bounded and cheaply checkable, a limited trial may be appropriate. Neither is a universal ban or blanket endorsement of AI.

Boundary: existing dispatch skills handle worker briefs and mechanics. This bundle decides whether delegation is justified and measures its value. It remains useful when the correct decision is to do the task directly.
