# Sameness and fatigue audit

Find the repeats a listener hears across hours that no single script review will show. Count first, judge second.

## Procedure
1. Fix the corpus: which segments, which kinds, which window. Leave out blocked, purged and draft items and say so. Compare like with like, talk with talk.
2. Measure from the text, not from impression: opening shape (first two words, and form: question, address to the listener, plain statement), closing shape, re-entry and transition lines, short reactions (about five words or fewer) counted by distinct wording, analogy frames ("think of it like", "imagine"), turn pattern (how often turns strictly alternate, how long one voice runs), line-length spread inside a segment, and phrases of four or more words that recur across segments.
3. Report each as n of N and a share. Calibrate against the shelf itself. A form used by roughly one segment in four or more, or a phrase recurring across more than a handful of segments, is a candidate fatigue item. These are starting heuristics, not rules, and you say so.
4. Separate ritual from fatigue. A registered sign-off or running bit is deliberate and rate-limited. The same hook in most openings, or three canned reactions making up most of the short lines, is accidental.
5. Check proximity. Two repeats an hour apart on air hurt more than the same two a week apart. Weight by how close they sit in the schedule when that is known.
6. Rank the top three repeats by share and proximity. Quote two examples each, choose a fix (rotate, retire, or a per-hour budget) and name the owner.

## Verification
Every claim shows its count and comes from a counting pass over the corpus. Thresholds are labelled starting heuristics. A minimum number of devices per segment is not a quality measure: it pushes every script towards one shape. Never recommend loosening a fact, safety or privacy gate to create variety. Re-measure after any change and report before and after.
